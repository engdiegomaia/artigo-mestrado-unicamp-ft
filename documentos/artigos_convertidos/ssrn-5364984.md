--- Página 1 --- Real-Time Multi-Rate Power System EMT Simulation on a Heterogeneous CPUGPU-FPGA Architecture Dehu Zou, Wei Gu, Wei Liu, Yang Cao, Mingwang Xu Abstract—Real-time Electromagnetic Transient (EMT) simulation of large-scale power systems with extensive power electronics presents a computational bottleneck. This paper introduces a novel multi-rate EMT simulation framework on a heterogeneous CPU-GPUFPGA architecture to address this challenge. The framework synergistically partitions the problem: the CPU simulates the main power grid at a larger time-step, the FPGA concurrently executes high-fidelity models of power electronic converters with submicrosecond resolution, and the GPU is dedicated to accelerating the computationally intensive update of the LU factorization of the admittance matrix following network topology changes. Case studies validate that the framework accurately captures fast converter transients while maintaining system-level real-time performance. This work offers a scalable, high-fidelity solution for the Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) analysis of modern power systems. However, applying such a small time-step across an entire large-scale grid is computationally intractable and fails to meet the realtime constraints required for system-level analysis [1]. Heterogeneous computing has emerged as a primary strategy to address this challenge, mapping computational tasks to the most suitable hardware. Nevertheless, the two prevailing heterogeneous architectures each present significant bottlenecks. On one hand, CPU-FPGA platforms[2-5] leverage the parallelism and extremely low latency of FPGAs for high-fidelity, small-step modeling of local power electronic devices. However, the limited on-chip resources and development complexity of FPGAs constrain their scalability for simulating entire grids[6]. On the other hand, CPU-GPU architectures[7-14] capitalize on the massive parallel processing power of GPUs to accelerate the large-scale matrix computations inherent in system-level EMT analysis. Yet, the data communication bandwidth and synchronization overhead between the CPU and GPU create a performance bottleneck, necessitating larger simulation time-steps that sacrifice the fidelity needed to capture fast transients. Consequently, no existing architecture effectively unifies high-fidelity modeling with large-scale, real-time performance within a single framework. A unified platform capable of multi-rate co-simulation—wherein different parts of a system run in parallel at their optimal physical time scales—remains a critical and unaddressed challenge. Overcoming this bottleneck is key to advancing Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing technology for modern power systems with a high penetration of power electronics. This paper addresses this gap by proposing and implementing a unified, multi-rate EMT simulation framework on a synergistic CPU-GPU-FPGA architecture. Traditional simulation frameworks, typically relying on a single computational resource such as a multi-core CPU or a single FPGA board, often struggle to manage complex, multi-rate tasks—particularly under the constraints of small time steps, high-frequency switching, and large-scale network modeling. The integration of heterogeneous computing resources, including CPUs, GPUs, and FPGAs, provides a pathway to surmount these challenges by exploiting the distinct computational advantages This preprint research paper has not been peer reviewed. In this architecture, tasks are partitioned according to hardware strengths. The CPU acts as the central orchestrator, managing the real-time clock and the main grid simulation. However, for large-scale networks, the LU factorization required after a topological change can easily exceed a typical 50 µs time-step, creating a bottleneck. To overcome this, the GPU, with its massively parallel architecture, is used to accelerate these computationally intense matrix operations. A full sparse LU factorization on a GPU introduces significant algorithmic complexity and irregular memory access patterns that can undermine performance. To address this challenge, we propose a hybrid, block-wise update strategy that leverages the GPU's strength in dense linear algebra while avoiding the overhead of a full sparse implementation. The strategy begins with a precise pre-ordering of the grid nodes. Prior to the simulation, the network nodes are reordered using the Constrained Approximate Minimum Degree (CAMD) algorithm, which is implemented in widely-used numerical libraries such as SuiteSparse CHOLMOD[15]. The CAMD algorithm permits the partitioning of nodes into ordered constraint sets. To achieve the desired matrix structure, all nodes associated with dynamic elements like circuit breakers (Set B) are assigned to a constraint set with a higher index than that of the static main grid nodes (Set A). Hardware optimization is key to this performance. Pipelined architecture is used to decompose complex tasks into stages, enabling new results every clock cycle. This, combined with the FPGA's parallel processing capabilities and the use of custom fixed-point arithmetic—which reduces computational complexity compared to standard floating-point formats—allows for extremely fast and accurate simulation of high-frequency converter dynamics. To bridge the high-fidelity FPGA simulation with the CPU-based main grid simulation in hard real-time, a high-performance communication link is essential. A custom, lightweight UDP-based protocol is implemented over a 10-Gigabit Ethernet link to manage this data exchange. As illustrated in the flowchart in Fig. 3, the protocol consists of two distinct phases: an initialization protocol for establishing the connection and exchanging parameters like the admittance matrix, and an operation protocol for the continuous, real-time transmission of boundary electrical quantities between the CPU and FPGA.
